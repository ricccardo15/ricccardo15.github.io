## TL;DR
UPM has no dormitories, so international students must find a room in Madrid's private market — fast, often in another language, and with real risk of scams. We interviewed 6 students, coded **615 sentences into 441 codes, 57 categories and 6 themes**, defined personas and tasks, and designed a **safer search → visit → contract** flow.

## My role
- **Interviews:** ran the 6 interviews with the team (consent, field notes, transcripts) and an individual contextual inquiry.
- **Analysis:** coded 2 of the 6 interviews in two independent rounds (intra-coder stability 87–92%), then reached consensus with my coding partner and the whole team.
- **Ethics:** informed consent and GDPR-compliant data handling.
- **Interaction design:** co-designed the layout scheme and the wireflows of the three key tasks.

## The problem
For newcomers, finding housing is one of the most stressful parts of moving abroad: high demand, fast turnover, unfamiliar platforms and scams. It shapes how they start their studies and their social life.

**How do international students actually search, decide and get through it — and where does it break?**

## Research
- **Planning with the 6 Ws** (who, what, why, where, when, how) and a semi-structured interview guide.
- **6 interviews** with international master's students from two continents who had just found a place in Madrid.
- **Coding in three steps:** each interview coded by two researchers in two rounds, then pair consensus (unanimity), then a group meeting to build the themes (majority).

The six themes follow the journey: context and prior experience → search strategy and decisions → tools and platforms → obstacles and support → money → outcome. The most frequent topics were the **current housing experience** and **platform use** (e.g. Idealista and social media): platforms are where opportunities and friction both happen.

We turned the findings into two personas, e.g. **Lukas**, an exchange student with no previous housing experience and almost no preparation before arriving.

## Ideas
Three tasks came out of the research as the critical ones:
1. **Search with filters for safety and availability**
2. **Book a visit** (a way to check that a listing is real)
3. **Sign the contract and pay securely**

## Result
A validated picture of the problem (with reliability and validity checks, following the COREQ checklist) and a first interaction concept focused on trust: verified visits before paying, and contract and payment in one guided flow.

## What I'd change
- **Agree a shared codebook before coding:** before consensus, my pair agreed on very few codes because we used different words for the same idea. A short codebook at the start would save hours.
- **Test the wireflow** with the same students before going further.
- **Add landlords' and agencies' point of view**, to design trust from both sides.
